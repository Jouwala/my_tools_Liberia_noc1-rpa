# Who gets the site-down ticket, and what actually decides it?

## One-liner

**Decided by the root cause (`root_cause`), NOT by the site-down type CSD / PSD; the reference point is the alarm-occurrence time (including the rule's built-in 4-hour delay), not the dispatch time.**

---

## Common Misunderstandings

### Misunderstanding 1: Thinking the site-down type decides who gets it

**Fact: the share dispatched to the third party is almost identical for CSD and PSD.**

| Site-down type | Tickets | To G:3rd_Power | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| CSD (Complete Site Down) | 14,292 | 8,202 | **57.4%** |
| PSD (Partial Site Down) | 2,882 | 1,651 | **57.3%** |

The two differ by 0.1 percentage point — **the site-down type has essentially no effect on dispatch ownership.**

### Misunderstanding 2: Thinking it's judged by the site state at "dispatch time"

**Fact: the reference point is the alarm-occurrence time. And the 4-hour delay is built into the rule, not a late dispatch.**

Hard evidence from the eFlow history-alarm API (`fm-history/v1/hisalarms/pageinfo`):

| Field | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `Assign RuleName` | **SITEOUTAGE_REGULAR_COOL** | Auto-dispatch rule (REGULAR=regular site, COOL=site power type) |
| `Assign Delay` | **14400s (= 4 hours)** | **built-in rule delay**, not an anomaly |
| `Assign Start Time` | 2026-08-22 06:28:06 | = alarm_occur_time (02:28:06) + 4h, **matches to the second** |
| `Assigned Type` | Assign-Append | Auto-append |

**Dispatch time = alarm time + rule delay (4h).** This is by design: give the network a 4-hour self-recovery window, dispatch only if not recovered. So challenging the dispatch with "it was dispatched hours late and the state had changed" is the wrong angle.

### Misunderstanding 3: Thinking 2G/3G went down simultaneously at alarm time (the actual timeline in this case)

All 4 alarms of site 3618 (Lango Number2) on 2026-08-22 (verified via the eFlow history-alarm API):

| Alarm | Standard | Occurred | Recovered | Relation to TT047 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SITE DOWN DUE TO POWER OFF | 2G | 08-22 **02:27:21** | 08-28 20:57:42 | Triggered TT047 |
| Complete site down (CSD) | 2G | 08-22 02:27:23 | 08-28 20:54:40 | Not linked |
| 2G SITE DOWN | 2G | 08-22 02:34:25 | 08-28 20:54:40 | Not linked |
| **3G SITE DOWN** | **3G** | **08-22 13:31:07** | **08-23 08:21:27** | Appended into TT047 via Assign-Append at 17:31 |

How to read it:

- **At alarm time (02:27) only 2G was down; 3G was fine then**
- **At dispatch time (06:28) 2G was still down, 3G hadn't even gone down yet** (3G dropped 7 hours later)
- 3G recovered next day at 08:21; 2G stayed down until 8/28 (= total ticket duration 6d18h30m)
- The TT's `impacted_service=2G,3G` is because the 3G alarm was later **appended** into this TT, **not the site-down state at alarm/dispatch time**

> In this case the local staff said three things: "2/3 went down together at alarm time", "3G was back up at dispatch time", "should be dispatched to us per PSD" —
> the first two contradict the alarm trace, and even if the third were true it wouldn't change ownership (see Misunderstanding 1).

## The Real Dispatch Basis: Root Cause

Whole-DB `eflow_tt` dispatch ownership by `root_cause` (data 2026-09-01):

| Root cause `root_cause` | Total | To 3rd-party power | To ZTE_NOC_FO | 3rd-party share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **POWER** | 14,432 | 10,547 | 1,702 | **73.1%** |
| Auto-recovery | 3,848 | 59 | 358 | 1.5% |
| RAN | 728 | **0** | 345 | **0%** |
| MW | 391 | **0** | 137 | **0%** |
| Fiber | 365 | **0** | 140 | **0%** |
| Uknown | 373 | 118 | 171 | 31.6% |
| Planned work | 111 | 65 | 30 | 58.6% |

**How to read it**:
- Root cause is **POWER** → most likely dispatched to third-party power group `G:3rd_Power`
- Root cause is **RAN / MW / Fiber** → **zero** sent to third party, all self-maintained

> ⚠️ About 27% of POWER-class tickets still go elsewhere, meaning root cause is not the only condition (REGULAR/COOL in the rule name are site-attribute dimensions). The exact rule is per the eFlow config-center dispatch rules.

## Case: TT202608220047 (Lango Number2) Full Conclusion

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| `root_cause` / `sub_root_cause` | POWER / Power Solar (solar battery depleted) |
| `ticket_severity` / `sla_status` | P3 / Failed |
| Dispatch rule | SITEOUTAGE_REGULAR_COOL, built-in delay 4h |
| Dispatched to | G:3rd_Power (third-party power group) |
| 2G down window | 08-22 02:27 → 08-28 20:57 (6d18h30m, full-risk duration) |
| 3G down window | 08-22 13:31 → 08-23 08:21 (dropped only after dispatch, recovered next day) |

**Not mis-dispatched.** Three independent lines of evidence:

1. **Root cause**: 73.1% of POWER-class go to third party, and this ticket's root cause is precisely POWER
2. **Rule**: SITEOUTAGE_REGULAR_COOL auto-dispatch, 4h built-in delay, dispatch time matches to the second
3. **Timeline**: reference point 02:27 (2G fully down + CSD alarm + power cause);
   3G's change happened after dispatch and logically could not affect the dispatch decision

**Talking points for local staff**: 4 hours is a system rule (waiting for self-recovery), not slow dispatch;
dispatch looks at the moment of the alarm (2G fully down, power cause), while 3G dropped only after dispatch and recovered next day;
the "2G,3G" on the TT was appended afterwards and does not represent the state at dispatch time.

## When facing a "mis-dispatched" complaint, check these three first

```sql
SELECT tt_no, alarm_occur_time, created_time, root_cause, sub_root_cause,
       root_cause_description, sub_alarm_name, last_processer_group
FROM eflow_tt WHERE tt_no = 'TT号';
```

1. **What is `root_cause`** — POWER-class to third party is normal
2. **How long between `alarm_occur_time` and `created_time`** — close to 4 hours is the rule delay, normal
3. **CSD/PSD in `sub_alarm_name`** — for reference only, does not determine ownership

One level deeper (query the real occurrence/recovery times of each standard's alarms):

```bash
python "D:\01_RPA\Task\eflow_Q&A\tools\eflow_hisalarm_by_tt.py" TT202608220047
```

The local `history_alarm` table only retains 48 hours rolling (`eflow_alarm_config.json: his_alarm_hours`);
for earlier alarms this tool connects directly to the eFlow API, pulls by time window, then filters by ticket number.
